# Brain Training Gaps

## Prospective memory

**What it is.** Prospective memory (PM) is the ability to remember to perform an intended action when a specific future trigger occurs — an event, a moment, a cue embedded in ongoing activity. It is distinct from retrospective memory (remembering *what* happened) and from working memory (holding something in mind right now). In a fast-paced work environment, PM failures look like: forgetting to follow up on something that came up in a meeting, missing the moment to mention something when the right person appears, or losing an intention between two tasks.

**Why it is not covered.** Every current bt task is reactive — you respond to the stimulus in front of you. None require you to hold a dormant intention across unrelated activity and fire it at the right moment.

**Paradigm.** Based on Einstein & McDaniel's event-based PM design:

1. Before the session starts, the player is given one or more *intentions* — "if you see shape X, do Y instead of the normal response."
2. The player then runs a fast ongoing task (classification, reaction, whatever is cognitively absorbing enough to occupy attention).
3. PM targets — stimuli matching the intentions — appear rarely (~10–15% of trials), embedded in the ongoing stream.
4. The player must keep performing the ongoing task well *and* catch every target without false-alarming on near-misses.

**Metrics.**

- **PM hit rate** — fraction of targets correctly caught.
- **Ongoing task cost** — RT slowdown on non-target trials vs a no-PM baseline (the monitoring-execution tradeoff: vigilance has a price).
- With multiple simultaneous intentions loaded, a **multi-intention accuracy** score.

**Difficulty levers.** Number of simultaneous intentions (1 → 2 → 3), PM target frequency (rarer = harder), ongoing task speed/load, focal vs non-focal targets (targets that look like ongoing-task stimuli are harder to catch than visually distinct ones).

**Direct work relevance.** This is the cognitive skill behind "keeping a flag on something while doing something else" — the gap between knowing you need to do X and actually doing X when the moment arises. It is underrepresented in brain training software despite having solid lab transfer evidence (Einstein & McDaniel, 2005).
